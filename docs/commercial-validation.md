# SecondCut — a workshop validation plan

**First customer:** a small leather maker repeating simple accessory batches from irregular offcuts. **First job:** decide whether a specific remnant fits requested rectangular panels or elliptical blanks, then prepare a plan that agrees with the physical material.

This is a hypothesis, not completed customer discovery. The initial product serves a narrower pattern scope than full leather CAD. Fabric remains an adjacent exploration because stretch, nap and print matching add constraints.

## The value to test

Start with planning time. Photo segmentation is only useful if upload, prompting, correction and scale verification take less effort than the maker's current method. Material recovery is a second question: did the plan produce usable pieces that otherwise would not have been cut, or prevent a real material purchase?

Geometry utilization does not equal waste avoided. User-entered piece value does not equal revenue or profit. An attractive screenshot proves neither.

## Discovery before a paid plan

Recruit three to five independent leather makers who repeatedly cut small accessories. Ask them to bring a normal order, its patterns and real offcuts. Observe their current process before showing SecondCut. Ask which steps cost effort, which materials they would never trust to a photo plan, and whether they already use tracing or nesting tools. Do not ask leading questions about how much they “love” the product.

For each maker, run at least five jobs spanning clean outlines, concave boundaries, holes, similar backgrounds and lighting changes. Include a job the material cannot satisfy. Document every failure, not just the demo-friendly example. Obtain permission before retaining photos and remove identifiable customer designs from public reports.

## Measurement sheet

| Measure | How to capture it | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total time | From raw material/photo to a physically checked plan, for current method and SecondCut. | Include prompting, correction, export and ruler checks. |
| Boundary error | Compare selected mask against a manually inspected material map. | Report over-inclusion separately because it risks invalid placements. |
| Scale discrepancy | Measure an exported 50 mm bar and physical test shape. | Include printer/import settings and camera setup. |
| Actual fit | Cut a low-cost test piece or physically position a verified template. | Reject a layout if the physical material disagrees. |
| Requested output | Count requested, placed, physically usable and rejected pieces. | Unplaced requests must remain visible. |
| Correction effort | Count prompts, exclusions, brush edits and retakes. | More automation can still mean more work. |
| Purchase outcome | Record a specific avoided purchase only when evidence supports it. | Do not assign cash savings to already-owned scrap automatically. |
| Repeat use | Invite a second independent job without assistance. | Initial curiosity is not retention. |

## Price and cost experiments

A **$9/month saved-job plan** is one unvalidated offer to test. Compare it with pay-per-export and free local use. Ask for an actual voluntary paid pilot after a demonstrated benefit. Existing free tools mean price alone is weak differentiation.

Track actual costs per active maker: model/runtime delivery, authenticated requests, database use, photo storage, support time and failed-job assistance. Browser inference removes per-call model API charges, not operating costs. Set quotas deliberately and avoid unlimited storage promises.

## Decision after the pilot

Continue if makers independently repeat the workflow, physically trust the checked output, and value it enough to pay or commit to a longer pilot. Improve capture or narrow materials if correction dominates. If simple blanks rarely need software, expand only after evidence identifies a valuable pattern task. No threshold, savings target or conversion rate here is an achieved outcome.

## Competitors to compare directly

- [UMWL](https://usemorewasteless.com/leather-hide-nesting/): manual hide mapping, quality zones, SVG parts and paid nesting exports.
- [ScrapFit](https://scrapfit.webbe-b.com/): local fabric remnant planning with manual geometry and defects.
- [Stitch Hero](https://stitch-hero.com/): leather CAD, photo calibration/tracing and nesting, free while in development.
- [Zünd](https://www.zund.com/en/applications/interiors/turnkey-systems-for-leather-processing): industrial camera capture and nesting/cutting systems.

Potential future advantage is better capture guidance, permissioned correction data and repeatable saved material/pattern workflows. Those assets do not yet constitute a moat.
